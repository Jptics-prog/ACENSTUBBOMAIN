Where this starts

Aerial inspection ends at the top of the module.Everything that fails starts underneath it.

01

The fault is invisible from above

A drone captures the top of the glass. Every terminal, every MC4, every junction box lives on the underside — the one surface no aerial pass, and no walk down the access road, ever actually sees.

02

Bushfire season sets the deadline

The short-term imperative is to identify the urgent thermal risks at Stubbo before the 2026 NSW bushfire season. One bad join can undo decades of work building social licence.

03

The clock is the money

The modules were bought directly and issued to PCL to install, so a hot connector is JA Solar's or Rizen's problem to fix — while the evidentiary bar for proving it is high enough that a phone photo from a walk-down rarely clears it.

04

One pass beats a campaign

A unit that inspects on every pass produces the same reference points every time — a comparable, dated, geolocated record instead of a single snapshot nobody can reproduce.

The cost of proving a warranty claim is a crew walking every row with a handheld camera. The value is a dated, geolocated thermal record, referenced to your own CBX and string identifiers — built while the manufacturer, not ACEN, is still on the hook.

Before handover

Thermal before Stubbo handover.After the window closes, the same findings are your cost.

Stubbo's as-built set is issued and dated. The defects liability period, and the warranties behind it, run on a clock, and the burden of proof sits with you. Connector and junction-box defects are exactly the class of fault that is latent at handover, invisible from the air, and expensive once it is yours.

A whole-site pass before that window closes converts a walk-down you cannot resource into a documented, geolocated defect register, referenced to the same drawing numbers your contractor works to.

ACEN's own operations team takes Stubbo over from Solarig on 17 December 2027. A baseline set now, on PCU-26, is the standard that's already proven on your own ground when that handover happens.

The obligation

In New South Wales, a hot DC connection is a work-health-and-safety matter, not just a warranty one.

The Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (NSW) and AS/NZS 5033 require DC wiring and connectors to be kept safe and inspected by licensed personnel. SafeWork NSW has run compliance checks at NSW solar farms since 2021, naming DC faults and fire risk explicitly.

Neither sets a threshold or an interval. The standard is whatever you can evidence — a dated, geolocated record of what was found, where, and when. Every pass leaves one automatically.
